Sixty seconds in the live apps.

Each step names the exact button and what you should see. Open BASECAMP in a new tab; it shows two replicas side by side, so one browser is enough. The times are a budget, not a measurement. If you see something else, that is a bug we want to hear about.

Potential Impact20% · 10 pointsA real problem for a real audience? Would someone actually use this?

For groups who share a list or a form where the signal drops (a trip, a site survey), and for the developers who build those apps. Last-write-wins quietly drops an edit; LIMBO shows the lost edit as a twin with the rule that decided it, and one tap settles it. Both apps keep working offline after the first load (tested), with no account. The scenarios are ours: there is no outside-user data yet.

the path, steps 3 to 5 · offline-shell.spec.ts · ADOPT.md (for developers)

Technological Implementation20% · 10 pointsHow thoroughly and skillfully does it use Kiro? Variety, strategy, experimentation?

12 Kiro-authored specs (179 spec sessions) among 194 headless Kiro CLI build sessions; 8 hooks with 1,824 logged runs, including an Agent Stop gate that refused "done" 2 times; 6 steering files; 2 custom agents; an MCP server; property-based tests at 10,000 orderings; a pre-registered A/B pilot, published with its null result.

Plate V, file by file · evidence/kiro/ · receipts

Design20% · 10 pointsA complete, coherent product experience? Intuitive, polished, intentional?

Every look is a sync state: fog while a replica's link is down, a count of waiting edits, a cyanotype ghost twin for a surfaced conflict, the FIXED seal when a replica is back to live with no open conflict. The Inspector lists the queued and recently exchanged ops with their rule and causal stamp. Two complete apps with an offline shell; the same look on the landing, the deck and this page.

BASECAMP · AFTERSHOCK · landing · 12-slide deck · Plate III

Innovation / Idea20% · 10 pointsHow creative and novel? Does it differ from existing concepts?

Last-write-wins, the common default, settles a conflict by silently keeping one edit. Here every op must declare one of 6 named merge rules, and the conflicts a person should see (an item deleted while it was edited, two claims on the last stove) come back as ghost twins with their rule printed on them. The merge code itself was gated: the Stop gate refused to let Kiro finish (up to 3 times a session) while a fast-tier property failed.

merges.ts · kit/props/ · the Stop gate

A 2:47 video: the ghost twin on the first frame, a judge-style edit of our own, the second app, an offline reload, Kiro and the Stop gate, the receipts. Then this page, a landing page and a deck, every number linked to its file.

plate X · honest limits

Honest limits, before you find them.

  1. Convergence is measured, not proven.10,000 random orderings of 3 replicas with property tests is strong evidence, not a proof.
  2. There is no cross-device relay.The live apps sync two replicas through an in-page link labelled as simulated. DevTools "offline" does not cut it; the dev-only relay in the e2e suite ships in no production build.
  3. Offline claims are provisional.No CRDT can stop two offline people both claiming the last stove. On reconnect the declared rule picks one claimant on every replica and the other is told.
  4. The kit showed no measurable effect.The pre-registered A/B pilot: 0 of 10 vs 0 of 10 held-out failures, p = 1. Its 8 kit-arm Stop blocks were false alarms from the kit's own vacuity guard, and 2 of 10 kit-arm runs edited that guard; the fix (the guard is now hash-protected) has not been re-piloted.
  5. Not every product commit is Kiro’s.9 of 124 product commits were made outside Kiro (listed with their subjects in share.json), and the ledger holds 17 hand rows in the ledger (16 edits made outside Kiro, 1 attribution correction), each with its reason.
  6. The Stop gate blocked twice in 194 build sessions.Once unplanned, once on a fault we seeded to prove it fires. It allows at most 3 blocks a session, and it skipped 19 times before the property harness existed.
  7. Kiro CLI only.No logged session used the Kiro IDE, its Spec Correctness (property-based) or the Agent Hooks panel, or Powers. The MCP server was used lightly: 8 calls from 1 session.
  8. The skeleton is over its first budget.21 files and 2,189 lines by the budget count: inside the restated 2,400-line budget, over the original 1,800.
  9. Demo data, no outside users.AFTERSHOCK runs on data we wrote and no field use is claimed; we have no outside-user data for either app.
  10. Only the apps work offline.The landing page and this page are static pages with no service worker.
